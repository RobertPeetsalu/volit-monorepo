## Voting on Proposed projects

Every voter can vote once in favour or against each project's each Proposed version. They can change their vote as long as the project version is in Proposed state, which means until this version becomes Enacted, Rejected or Expired (by reaching its voting deadline, if any). 

Each Proposed, Enacted, In Implementation, Implemented, Rejected and Expired project version displays a count and an approximate percent of votes in favour and against it. The percent is the ratio of votes in favour or against this version to the total count of eligible voters in the administrative districts that the project version is scoped to. In case the project version is in the Proposed state, use the latest counts of eligible voters in each district, requested regularly through the statistics bureau integration. If the project version is no longer Proposed, then the ratio should use the last counts received before the time the project version transitioned from Proposed to Rejected, Expired or Enacted state, which is when the voting results where locked. 

### Voting on local problems and projects

**In case of problems and projects scoped to one or more administrative divisions** (as defined in the problem's Current version or the project's Proposed version)**, only voters registered as residents in one of these administrative divisions or holding delegations from such residents are eligible to prioritize such problems, vote on such Proposed project versions and their reviews and approve or disapprove of changes to these problem or project versions.** For all other problems and projects (those scoped at the treaty or country level), all of the country's voters are eligible to vote. 

Constraining the number of eligible voters to local residents allows even the smallest community level initiatives (e.g. to build some benches in a park) to gather enough support and get implemented quickly. 

For the app to be able to constrain voting to only voters registered in an administrative division, the government has to provide a service to digital identity wallets that are used to authenticate with the application. This service has to return a credential verifying the wallet holder's current residence at the most granular level of administrative division that retains anonymity. The wallet used for authentication has to be able to generate an anonymous version of the credential, verifying the holder's residence to the application. 

### Free legal review on projects that are gaining traction

If a Proposed version of a Legislative or Institutional project gains support of e.g. over 0.01% of voters (ratio set by law), then a government employed lawyer is assigned to review it and post a "Legal" type review within a set number of workdays with references to remaining unresolved legal conflicts. This review informs voters on whether this Proposed version would require a majority or a supermajority of votes to be Enacted and what would be the resulting legal changes. 

### Educating voters 

When a citizen contributes for the first time to a problem or project (with a commit, vote, approval/disapproval, review or reaction), a dialogue window could open, where a not overly confident language model asks some questions that show the citizen's understanding of core causal relations relevant to the problem or project. If the citizen answers that they don't know or asks for explanations, then the language model explains these relations as well as terms that are frequently misunderstood and corrects the user's answer to help them form an informed decision. This way the average voter could be better informed on the basics before casting their vote or contributing. It could increase the quality of projects, reviews and votes and even allow lowering the voting age limit. More research is needed to see if it would in fact have these benefits though.
